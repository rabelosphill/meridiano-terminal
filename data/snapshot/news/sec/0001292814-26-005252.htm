Cash In the Exploration & Production segment, Capex totaled US$ 5.3 billion in 3Q26, a 23.2% increase compared to 2Q26. The higher investment level mainly reflected progress in the construction of the Búzios 9 (P-80), Búzios 10 (P-82), Atapu 2 and Seap 2 FPSOs, as well as the intensification of the well connection campaign at Búzios 8 (P-79), which started production in May 2026. Compared to 3Q25, Capex increased by 14.4%, driven by higher investments in major pre-salt projects in Santos Basin, especially the new production system for Atapu 2 and progress in the well construction campaigns for Búzios 9 and 10. Also noteworthy were the sail away of FPSOs P-80, on September 30, and P-82, on October 18, from Singapore towards the Búzios field, in Santos Basin pre-salt, Brazil's largest field in terms of reserves and production. Each FPSO has the capacity to produce 225 thousand barrels of oil per day and process 12 million cubic meters of gas per day. The units are part of a group of six high-capacity FPSOs destined for the Búzios field. Both are expected to start production in 2027. In the Refining, Transportation and Marketing segment, Capex totaled US$ 0.89 billion in 3Q26, a 32.1% increase compared to 2Q26, mainly due to higher investments at the Boaventura Hub, Nitrogen Fertilizer Unit III (UFN-III), scheduled maintenance stoppages at RPBC, REDUC and REPAR refineries, and maintenance of Transpetro's infrastructure. Compared to 3Q25, Capex increased by 46.8%, mainly reflecting higher investments at the Boaventura Hub, Abreu e Lima Refinery (RNEST), and maintenance of Transpetro's infrastructure. In the Gas and Low Carbon Energies segment, Capex totaled US$ 0.10 billion in 3Q26. Compared to 2Q26, Capex decreased by 22.3%, mainly reflecting the acquisition of an interest in Lightsource bp in the previous quarter (US$ 0.03 billion), as well as lower investments in the maintenance of natural gas assets. Compared to 3Q25, Capex decreased by 1.1%, remaining close to the same level. 10 Capex Revision of Capex Guidance We have revised our total Capex guidance (Portfolio Under Implementation) for 2026 from US$ 19.4 billion (+/- 5%) to a range of US$ 24 billion to US$ 25.5 billion, while our cash Capex guidance (Portfolio Under Implementation) was revised from US$ 16.9 billion (+/- 5%) to a range of US$ 21 billion to US$ 22 billion. It is worth noting that the guidance revision does not represent projects’ capex overrun, but mainly reflects foreign exchange effects and better-than-planned project execution. Key factors include better-than-expected progress in the construction of Petrobras-owned FPSOs for the Búzios, Atapu and Sépia fields, greater availability of drilling rigs, mostly allocated to Búzios projects, and an increase in the number of well connections, all of which have been contributing to production growth. Additionally, the Capex revision includes the expected nationalization of Búzios 9 and 10 FPSOs in 2026. Regarding cash Capex, the revision also takes into account the payment of signature bonuses related to the ANP bid rounds held in October 2026, which is expected to take place in December 2026. Liquidity and capital resources As of September 30, 2026, cash and cash equivalents totaled US$ 6.9 billion, and adjusted cash and cash equivalents amounted to US$ 12.1 billion. In 3Q26, cash generated from operating activities reached US$ 12.0 billion, and free cash flow totaled US$ 6.6 billion. This level of cash generation, combined with the funds raised throughout 3Q26, was primarily used to: (a) carry out investments (US$ 5.4 billion); (b) amortize lease liabilities (US$ 2.7 billion); (c) amortize principal and interest due during the period (US$ 2.1 billion); and (d) remunerate shareholders (US$ 1.6 billion). In 3Q26, the company settled several loans and financings totaling US$ 2.1 billion, notably the repurchase and redemption of US$ 1.2 billion in bonds issued in the international capital markets. During the same period, the company raised US$ 1.2 billion in funding in the international banking market. In 3Q26, operating cash flow (OCF) was negatively impacted by working capital effects amounting to US$ 2.5 billion. This impact mainly reflected an increase in accounts receivables (US$ 1.7 billion), including amounts related to the fuel subsidy program, and inventories (US$ 1.2 billion). The increase in inventories was primarily driven by higher crude oil inventories, associated with production growth and underway exports at the end of the quarter. These effects were partially offset by a positive change of US$ 0.4 billion in trade payables, as purchases of imported crude oil and oil products on credit exceeded payments made during the period.
